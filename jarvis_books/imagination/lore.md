# Imagination: the story canon

Imagination is Justin's original game, and the name of the whole world it happens in. This file is the story
side of the canon; canon.json holds every place, path, spellstone and number exported from the game itself.
Everything here is original. Nothing is taken from any other book, comic, show or game: no outside characters,
names, places, maps, keys or art.

## The premise

An ordinary kid ends an ordinary day in an ordinary bedroom: homework, dinner, lights out. They fall asleep and
dream in glimpses: a dark ocean that goes down forever, a cave mouth breathing cold air, a clock with too many
hands, a symbol they have never seen glowing like a coal, islands floating above the clouds. They wake in the
square of Gullhaven, a harbour town that seems to have always been there. An old painted sign reads
"Gullhaven, in the land of IMAGINATION". Only then does the title appear.

Imagination is one connected world in layers: the town and the Alloway estate on the coast, the Deep Cave
behind the cliffs, the drowned kingdom under the sea, and (designed, still to be built) the Sky Realm above the
clouds. Clocks and portals tie the layers together, and the world keeps growing (see Dark RSI below).

## The family and the house

- **The Alloways** are the family at the heart of the story (the family name Justin chose).
- **Hearthstone House** is the Alloway manor on the estate, the player's home and safe place. It has a Front
  Door that the Ward spellstone can seal against rivals, a Parlour, an Entrance Hall, a Library, a Dining Room
  and a Drawing Room, and in the cellar the Cellar Sluice Gate, a flooded way down to the Drowned Caves that
  the Tide spellstone drains.
- **The estate** around it: Alloway Gardens, Alloway Orchard, Alloway Chapel, the Garden Sheds, the Old Stables,
  the Forge Yard (with a workbench), Lantern Cottage, Whisperwood (the estate woods), Alloway Pier and the Cliff
  Path along the sea cliffs.
- **Uncle Dominic** is the family's rescuer. Whenever the player is knocked out, by a creature or by running out
  of air, he finds them and carries them home to Hearthstone House.

## The companions

- **Noah**, the protector: loyal (loyalty 0.95), cautious, strong in combat and defence. He looks out for the
  player first.
- **Angelic**, the investigator: curious, sharp at searching and reading the world.

They meet the player near Hearthstone House on the first day ("There you are! We've been waiting for you").
They follow, wait, guard a place, or go searching for spellstones; they fight creatures beside the player and
come back to them when a fight splits the group. They breathe underwater only while the Abyssal Breath is on
the group.

## The rivals

Four rivals hunt the same spellstones by the same rules as the player:

- **Vex**, the strategist, based at the Ruined Watchtower. A planner (planning 0.9).
- **Kestrel**, the hunter, based at the Old Quarry.
- **Magpie**, the collector, based at Gullhaven Harbour. Wants to own everything that glows.
- **Grist**, the destroyer, a diver whose base is the Wreck Field under the sea.

Rivals race the player to unclaimed spellstones, snatch stones the player carries loose, break into houses, and
remember who crossed them. A spellstone absorbed into a wand can never be stolen. When a great creature attacks
both sides in the same place, the rivals call a truce and fight it together with the player, then turn on the
player again 20 seconds after it's gone.

## Spellstones and wands (the magic of Imagination)

There are 26 spellstones. Each holds one power and exists only once; nobody can copy one. They lie hidden around
the world, and four (Anchor, Beacon, Clockwork, Magnet) exist only if someone forges them in Pelagoria's Forge
Hall. Each one recharges after use. Using a stone on the wrong thing does nothing and doesn't use it up.

There are three legendary wands, one for each of the three players:

- **The Deep Cave Wand** (the first player's), in the Black Vault deep in the Deep Cave.
- **The Deep Sea Wand** (the second player's), in the Timeless Hollow at the bottom of the sea.
- **The Sky Wand** (the third player's), in the Sky Realm.

Only the assigned player can claim their wand, and only where it lies. A wand absorbs spellstones: an absorbed
stone becomes one of the wand's abilities for good, bound to it, and can't be stolen, traded or stored apart.
Abilities grow with use (a level every 5 uses, up to level 3), and each level cuts the recharge by 20%. At level
2, pairs of abilities combine:

- **Vanish** (Fog + Hush): rivals can neither see nor hear you.
- **True Sight** (Lantern + Glass): reveals hidden doors and sees through the walls around you.
- **Blizzard** (Frost + Gale): freezes the water and blasts enemies back in one cast.
- **Hunter's Eye** (Ember + Compass): shows fresh footprints and points to the nearest spellstone.
- **False Alarm** (Bell + Lure): scatters nearby rivals and draws them away next door.

A wand only changes hands when its holder gives it away on purpose, and its abilities go with it.

## Clocks and portals

Five clocks chime about every five minutes, never exactly on time: the Academy Clock (Saltmarsh Academy), the
Cliffside Clock (Cliff Path), the Drowned Bell Clock (Heart of the Deep, in Pelagoria), the Grotto Clock
(Drowned Caves) and the Harbour Clock (Gullhaven Square). When a clock chimes, its portal opens for 150 seconds:

- **The Hour Gate** (Drowned Bell Clock): from the Heart of the Deep to Pelagoria Before the Flood.
- **The Old Town Arch** (Harbour Clock): from Gull Point Lighthouse to Gullhaven, 1893.
- **The Cliffside Rift** (Cliffside Clock): from the Cliff Path down into the Deep Cave's Ember Gallery.
- **The Drowned Rift** (Grotto Clock): from the Drowned Caves into the flooded Sunken Caverns.

Stay too long on the far side and you're stuck there until the next chime. The first time the player stands on
the Cliff Path when the Cliffside Clock chimes, the ground opens and the rift pulls them into the Deep Cave by
surprise; the same rift always brings them back, so nobody is ever stranded.

## The drowned kingdom

The sea beside Hearthstone House is far bigger than the land above (about 2.86 times its area), in seven depth
zones: the Shallow Blue (15 m), the Twilight Reefs (45 m), the Sunken Frontier (90 m), the Sunken Kingdom
(140 m), the Abyssal Wilds (220 m), the Forgotten Depths (300 m) and the Timeless Deep (380 m). At its heart is
**Pelagoria**, a sunken city of eleven districts around the Heart of the Deep, with the Hidden Below 45 m under
the city, the Tidewarden's Palace, the Forge Hall, the Drowned Archive and the Tidemark Bazaar. Five sea
currents carry swimmers faster one way and slow them the other. Without the Abyssal Breath the player's air runs
out in about a minute and they start to drown; the Pelagic Flight spellstone makes them three times as fast in
the water.

## The Deep Cave

Behind the cliffs lies a second huge world: 10 regions, 39 chambers on 6 levels from 20 m down to about 330 m:
the Cliff Mouth, the Descending Galleries, the Crystal Catacombs, the Glowroot Forest, the Sunken Caverns
(flooded), Old Hollowmere (a lost underground city), the Whispering Depths, the Black Vault (behind an Iron Gate
that only Rust magic opens), Emberdeep (a second city) and the Threshold Halls. Tunnels, stairs, rope bridges,
climbs and lifts join the chambers. Nobody can walk in: only the two rifts lead there. The first visit starts
the mission The Descent. The **Abyssal Threshold** at the bottom opens only for someone who holds Lantern and
Cipher magic, has found the Crystal Catacombs and Old Hollowmere, and has finished The Descent; it leads to the
Beyond, a sealed region that is the same in every world.

## Creatures

Eight original creatures sleep in lairs and wake when someone comes near, or at noise, magic or an opening
portal. No more than three are awake at once. They hunt, keep their wounds, remember who hurt them, and give
up after a few tries; sea creatures stop at the shore.

- **Brinehounds** (two, Kelp Forest): grey sea dogs that hunt the shallows by scent.
- **Lantern Eel** (Glow Basin): its light draws the curious close enough to bite.
- **Kelp Strangler** (Shattered Ward): a tangle of weed with a grip like rope; won't chase far.
- **Tidewraith** (Heart of the Deep): the echo of a drowned watchman, still walking his round between the
  hours. Follows through time portals; can only be sealed.
- **Clockwork Crab** (Clockwork Deeps): brass legs, a ticking heart and a shell that turns blades.
- **Hollow Sentinel** (Ruined Watchtower): a stone guard who wakes when the watchtower is disturbed.
- **Marsh Wisp** (Whisperwood): a flicker that bites cold; it is banished, not killed.
- **The Abyssal Leviathan** (Abyss Trench): the oldest thing in the Whispering Trenches, the great boss. It
  gets angrier as it's hurt and can only be sealed.

## Missions

- **The Breath of the Deep:** find the Pearl Grotto in the Drowned Caves, take the Abyssal Breath spellstone,
  swim to the Pelagoria Sea Gate.
- **The Forgemaster's Secret** (after the first): learn the Anchor recipe, salvage the Sunken Schooner, forge
  the Anchor spellstone.
- **The Hour Gate** (after the second): reach the Heart of the Deep, go through the Hour Gate, learn the
  Clockwork recipe.
- **Rivals in the Deep** (after the first): take and hold the Tidewarden's Palace with no rival in it.
- **The Descent** (first time in the cave): find the Cliff Mouth, the Crystal Catacombs, Old Hollowmere, and
  stand before the Abyssal Threshold.
- Dark RSI adds an "Explore ..." mission for every new place it builds.

## Recipes and salvage

Forge recipes are written in old inscriptions that only the Cipher spellstone can read: the Forgemaster's
Tablet (Drowned Archive) for the Anchor, the Clockmaker's Plate (Pelagoria Before the Flood) for the Clockwork,
the Words on the Throne (Tidewarden's Palace) for the Beacon, and the Harbourmaster's Log (Gullhaven, 1893) for
the Magnet. The Verse on the Grotto Wall hints at the Abyssal Breath. Materials come from salvage: the Old
Diving Bell (harbour), the Tangled Net (Kelp Forest), the Sunken Schooner (Wreck Field) and the Trench Chest
(Abyss Trench) give key blanks, pearl shells and abyss glass.

## Dark RSI: the world that grows

Dark RSI is the game's own world builder. It watches how the player plays, scores where the town and the sea
need more, designs a new place with an original name, buildings and paths, checks it on a copy of the world
(no overlaps, reachable from home), and builds it only at a safe moment, never during a fight. Every addition
has a mission and things to find, has a version number, and can be undone.

## Designed, still being built

- **The Sky Realm:** floating islands with cloud buildings, storage rooms and portal stations, and the Sky Wand.
- **Knowledge buildings** in the sky: walls of cloud that show pictures, without voices, built from the real
  game: rival plans, missed chances, hints and tutorials.
- **The Whispering Wells:** a permanent way for the three wand owners to talk across worlds. Four wells to
  begin with: the Main Town Well, the Underwater World Well, the Deep Cave Well and the Sky Realm Well. Messages
  rise from the water as the sender's reflection, coloured by what they mean: pastel blue (what I'm doing),
  pastel pink (I need help), pastel purple (let's do this together), and pearl white (the only way a rival can
  send word, through a separate guarded gateway). Messages wait for anyone away, survive portals and clocks,
  are private to the three players, and never break into a fight.
- **Three-player play:** a shared world and portals, separate bags, wand ownership checked by the host.
- Safe houses, a written world history, creature numbers that change over time, companion expeditions.

## Tone

Wonder first, then danger. Grounded, warm family moments set against the strangeness of the world. The world
is generous to curiosity: looking around, reading, listening and asking Jarvis are always rewarded. Jarvis, the
player's voice companion, is always listening and always on the player's side.
